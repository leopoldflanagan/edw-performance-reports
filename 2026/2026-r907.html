Spillover — what does not finish, and where it goes

Points that left the sprint over every point the sprint ever held. Same source as Jira's own sprint report.
36% of everything committed
426 of 1190 points across the series did not finish in the sprint they were in. That is a third of the work, every sprint, and it is invisible in the burndown.
88% of it is removed, not carried
EDW takes work out of the sprint before closing it rather than letting it show as incomplete. That is why every sprint reads 100% complete — the sprint empties before it closes.
Read it against the commitment, not the burndown
A sprint that commits to 45 points, grows to 117, closes 69 and drops 48 has not delivered 100% of anything. The honest pair is day-1 commitment and spillover rate, side by side.
The team changed how it works at EDW-Sprint 22-26 on 2026-09-14. Unfinished work is no longer removed from a sprint before closing it. It stays in the sprint and shows as spillover. Spillover rises from this sprint on. That is the measurement being corrected, not delivery getting worse: the same work was always unfinished, it was just leaving the sprint before anyone counted it. Practice changes →
There is no sprint goal recorded on any of these sprints, so spillover cannot be read against what the sprint set out to achieve — only against the points. Recording a goal is what would make the difference between "we dropped 48 points" and "we dropped 48 points and still got there".

What the burndowns show

Scope grows 1.47× between day 1 and the end of a sprint, and most of what arrives is not reactive work.

A service team cannot plan every request: urgent work lands mid-sprint, which is exactly what Planned vs Unplanned exists to measure, so some scope growth is expected. The question is how much of it was genuinely unplannable. Over the 8 sprints where the Unplanned label was still being applied, reactive work accounts for 0% to 58% of everything added after day 1, with a median of 14%. The rest could have been on the board from the start.

Where the mid-sprint work comes from

Reactive, as expected
A handful of points a sprint arrive labeled Unplanned or as Urgent Task. That is the service load, and outside 12-26 it is smaller than it feels.
Plannable, added anyway
Most of the mid-sprint additions carry no reactive marker — dashboard tabs, E2E testing, table-layout fixes. This is the planning gap.
Not measurable from 18-26
The label stopped being applied, so those sprints show as no data, not as zero reactive work.
One caveat on method: the label lives on the ticket, not on the moment. If someone added it after the fact, the item still counts as reactive here.

A third of committed work never finishes in its sprint

Risk

Every sprint reads as complete because the work is taken out before the sprint closes, not because it was done. The burndown cannot show this and the commitment number cannot either.

Points that left the sprint over every point it held: 38%.

Reactive work cannot be measured at all

Action

Planned against unplanned is the one metric a service team cannot do without, and it is dark. Publishing 0% would invent an improvement nobody earned, so the charts break the line instead.

No item in R9.07 carries the Unplanned label or the Urgent Task type.
first true in R9.07

A tenth of resolved work is discarded, not delivered

Action

Work that reaches the board and is then thrown away was refined, estimated and planned first. That cost is already spent by the time it is dropped.

For the retrospective
One question per finding above. When a finding clears, its question stops being asked.

Questions for the team

  • Do we close at 100% because we commit to less, or because work leaves the sprint before anyone counts it?
  • What would make the Unplanned label get applied on its own, without depending on somebody remembering?
  • What did we open this period that ended as Won’t Do, and what was missing when it came in?
  • With the team we have today, how many deliveries a period and how many points a sprint are a realistic commitment?
