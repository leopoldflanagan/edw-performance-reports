What the reports could also do

Four additions to the EDW delivery reports, running on the board’s real numbers so they can be judged rather than imagined. Nothing here is published and nothing writes to Jira or Confluence. Every figure comes from the same sources the live reports use; where a number is an assumption, the page says so and lets you move it.
Forecast the backlog, not the sprint
The live page projects how the running sprint closes. Nobody can ask it the question stakeholders actually ask: when do I get the thing I asked for? This answers it from the team’s own history — no velocity target, no estimate of the item, no promise. Ten thousand simulated futures, and the spread between them is the answer.

The clock the person who asked is watching
Cycle time starts when an item enters In Development. The person who filed it started counting when they filed it. Everything between those two moments — grooming, prioritising, waiting — is invisible in every chart the reports draw today.

Two clocks on the same work

Same items, two start lines. The team’s clock is the one already on the reports; the request’s clock is created to closed.

Findings that expire on their own
The Findings & Retro tab is prose written for one period. When the next release closes it still talks about the last one, and nothing tells anybody. Here each finding is a rule over the data: it appears when it becomes true, carries the release it first appeared in, and disappears when the team fixes it. A finding that has survived four releases is a stronger statement than any paragraph.

Rules that are not firing

The other half of the idea. A rule that stopped being true is the only evidence a team ever gets that something they changed worked.
Did the thing we changed work?
The capacity page already records what the team changed about how it works, and from which sprint. One column is missing: which number it was supposed to move. With that, the report can read the before and the after by itself, two periods later, and say whether it happened. This is the shortest path from a retro to evidence, and almost no team has it.

What this needs from you

Nothing technical. Two extra columns on the Practice changes table that already exists.
ColumnWhat goes in itWhy the report needs it
MetricOne of the numbers the reports already compute — spillover rate, items per sprint, cycle time median, unplanned share. So the report knows which series to read. Without it, it can mark the charts but not grade the change.
Expected directionUp, down, or steady. So a result can be wrong. A change with no expected direction cannot fail, which means it cannot teach anybody anything.
The rest is already there: the sprint the change took effect and what changed, both of which the reports read today to annotate the charts.
